### 1. Executive verdict

**Can today's architecture deliver the vision?** Yes for receive, use,
return and share. No for "modify and keep", which needs one redesign. On
iPhone durability, unknown and at risk. The architecture is not boxed in.
The corrections are narrow, but one of them is a real design change, and
the iPhone question can only be settled on a device.

**What holds.** The base layers are sound and tested on all three engines:
- the signed container
- verification before mount
- re-shelling on every hosted open
- the opaque-origin app frame (`sandbox="allow-scripts allow-forms"`,
  `src/runtime/bootloader.ts:3463`)
- object-first routing
- autosave with a host acknowledgment
- the two-tab lock
- clean-by-construction "Send the app"

The evidence:
- **F.** Main's gate is green. `node scripts/ci-verdict.mjs 38185274918`
  prints `gate: green on chromium, webkit and every checks job`.
- **F.** A local run of 39 V1-walk specs gave `168 passed` on
  Chromium+node and `150 passed` on WebKit, with no failures and no flakes.
- **I.** Within the personal-app scope, no route from app code to a
  host-owned operation was found (traced; Goal 6).

**Where it fails.** The product meets four facts the architecture has not
absorbed.

1. **Modification belongs to the publisher, not the person.**
   - **F.** A successor is adopted only when it is signed by the key this
     device pinned for its predecessor (`main.ts:4892-4900`).
   - A recipient never holds that key. So product steps 7–8, "ask AI to
     improve it; the improvement keeps my records", are refused by
     construction.
   - **F.** Meanwhile "Change something…" tells the person "the new version
     will replace this one and keep your data" (`main.ts:4775-4776`).
   - **F.** No classifier exists. The only path for an additive column is
     migration SQL the AI writes, which runs in the app frame
     (`bootloader.ts:2468-2483`). Ruling 8 rejected exactly that.
   - **This is the one redesign:** who may sign a successor of *my copy*,
     staged where, and accepted on which host sheet.
2. **A personal app's records are not signed.**
   - **F.** `document.sqlite` is excluded from `signedEntries`
     (`src/core.ts:685`).
   - **Ran.** A `tasks` build with its database replaced and its hash
     updated still gives `dai verify`: `intact … signature valid`.
   - So ruling 3's "recorded history … independently verifiable" holds for
     replicated rows only. The V1.0 default is plain tables.
   - The same fact leaves ruling 5's "linked successor under a new key"
     with nothing to link: `supersedes` is a publisher field.
3. **On iPhone, "kept in this browser" means two stores, and neither is
   shown to be durable.**
   - **F.** Safari and a Home Screen install are separate stores (ruling 4;
     `apps/runner/src/install.ts:28-33`).
   - **F.** The keep sheet moves the person from the first to the second
     after their first use and promises "works without a connection, and
     stays on this device" (`install.ts:599`).
   - **F.** The new icon opens the arrival snapshot, not the Safari work
     (`main.ts:1601`).
   - **A.** Not verified: that `persist()` protects a Safari tab from
     WebKit's seven-day deletion of script-written storage.
   - **F.** No device reading of an offline reopen or a seven-day gap is
     recorded.
   - **F.** Both WebKit offline tests are skipped (`tests/offline.ts:19-22`).
4. **The ordinary AI path loses data silently, and the no-tools hand-over
   is broken.** This is the cold probe, n=1, in §5.
   - **Ran.** A conventional localStorage app builds with exit 0.
   - **Ran.** The opener accepts that app, it takes input, and on reload
     the input is gone ("item present after reload: false") with no
     sentence shown.
   - **Ran.** The website's paste box approves a bundle in the recipe's own
     format, then builds a file that fails with `no such table: trip`.
   - **Ran.** With the model file in context, the same request produced an
     app that checks clean, builds, and persists.

**Targeted correction or substantial redesign?** Targeted correction,
except successor authority (item 1). Signing personal history (item 2) may
also need a format field. Both are narrow: one host branch, one sheet, one
signed field. Neither touches the container, the frame boundary, the merge,
or the router.

**The three highest-risk assumptions.**

1. A browser alone keeps one person's working copy on an iPhone across the
   gaps a personal app really has (days, weeks, a trip). Device evidence
   today: none.
2. A person's AI revision can carry their records forward under today's
   signing model. Today: refused by code.
3. An ordinary assistant, asked in ordinary words, produces something that
   keeps what is typed into it. Today: only with the 146,906-byte model file
   in context, which a recipient cannot find from the opener (§5).

**What to stop.**
- New work on V1.1 machinery:
  - seats, rosters, `_rows`, floors, the mailbox
  - merge vectors, the Rust reader, P0–P2 extensions
  - the chess reopen flake
- Hardening of replicated documents ahead of review 11.

Keep all of it in CI unchanged, but build nothing new there until Gate B.

- **F.** It is 17–21% of source, 32% of spec lines, and about 28 minutes of
  every CI run for P0–P2 alone (run 38185274918: properties-node 20.0 min,
  properties-python 7.8 min).
- None of it is on the V1.0 walk.

Also stop treating D248 as "re-shell on open": that is done.

**Are we architecturally ready to build the example? Conditional go** (§8).
- **Gate A is five items.** Four are written decisions and one is a
  delivery rule. With them done, the receive/use/return slice can be built
  on today's foundation.
- **The modify-and-keep half cannot be built** until successor authority is
  designed. Start that design now, beside the example. It is the long pole.

### 3. Architectural contradictions, ranked

Ranked by severity times the cost of correcting it late.

1. **No accounts and browser-only, against continuity on iPhone** (brief
   items 1, 2, 3). *Fundamental.*
   - Each iOS context has its own store (`install.ts:28-33`).
   - Links do not reach an installed icon (backlog 6.3).
   - A Safari tab is subject to WebKit's seven-day deletion.
   - With no account and no key export (ruling 4), the only bridge across
     contexts, or across a deletion, is a file the person holds.
   - **What cannot hold together:** "the same link tomorrow, work intact"
     and "no account, browser only", for every gap and context on iOS.
   - **Alternatives:**
     - Icon only: durable, but links do not reach it.
     - Safari only: links work; durability is unknown.
     - Encrypted backup to a store: a dependency, deferred.
     - A native host: D16, deferred.
   - **Smallest change:**
     - Pick one iOS context for V1.0, and word the keep sheet for it.
     - Promise continuity per context.
     - Make the dated backup the visible bridge (ruling 6's fourth state).
     - Take the device readings before any "ready offline" word.
   - **Cost of late correction:** the highest. It is the product sentence.
2. **User-controlled AI modification, against verifiable integrity** (item
   6). *Implementation-specific.*
   - Integrity today means "the pinned publisher key signed it"
     (`main.ts:4892`). The person never holds that key.
   - The format has room for this: a countersignature slot at label 11, and
     a host-held person key (`apps/runner/src/person.ts`).
   - **Alternative:** a successor signed by this context's person key.
     - It is adoptable only through a host sheet ("activate another
       release", ruling 9).
     - It must supersede a document held here.
     - It is shown as this person's change.
     - The publisher's signature on the predecessor is untouched.
   - **Smallest change:** a second branch in `planSuccession`, plus a host
     sheet and a classifier.
   - **Cost of late correction:** high. Every successor in the wild is made
     under whatever this rule turns out to be.
3. **"Recorded history is independently verifiable", against unsigned
   personal data.** *Implementation-specific.*
   - Data is outside the signed set by design (`core.ts:685`).
   - **Alternatives:**
     - Narrow the sentence to "the packaged app is verifiable; your records
       travel with it". Free.
     - A person-key signature over each saved database's digest. This is a
       format field, and it is what ruling 5's successor would point at.
     - Run personal tables through single-author replication. This brings
       seats into personal apps; rejected by contradiction 6 below.
   - **Smallest change:** narrow the sentence now. Decide the snapshot
     signature together with item 2, since both are "what the person key
     signs".
4. **Independent portability, against a central opener** (item 5).
   *Implementation-specific.*
   - Every working copy lives in opendai.app's origin storage. Every icon
     and `#a=` link is an opendai.app URL.
   - `#a=` is rebuilt from the receiving opener's retained host bytes
     (`src/inline.ts:9-20`; 92 under `apps/runner/public/hosts/`).
   - `/d/` depends on the project's store, which deletes after 90 days.
   - `?open=` and files need no project infrastructure.
   - Verification is fully independent: two readers and the CLI.
   - On iPhone, running a file needs the opener.
   - **Smallest change:**
     - Write down a policy on the origin's permanence and on retaining
       host bytes.
     - Keep `?open=` and files as the escape route.
     - Say that a `/d/` link expires and the copy does not.
   - **Cost of late correction:** grows with every link and icon in the
     wild.
5. **Offline ownership, against automatic recovery after deletion** (item
   3). *Incompatible by design; already ruled.*
   - Ruling 11 rejected "promising data survives deletion everywhere".
   - The remaining gaps: an undetectable full wipe (`identity.md:96`), and
     D51's sentence, which can be false.
   - **Smallest change:** the dated backup, and honest words.
6. **Simple one-person apps, against replication and sessions** (item 9).
   *Contained in storage, leaking into code paths.*
   - **Ran.** A personal container has:
     - `replication: undefined`
     - no `_dai_` tables, no `_rows` views, no triggers
     - only `_dai_meta`
   - **F.** The merge module is never sent (`main.ts:3092`), and `mayLeave`
     returns early (`main.ts:3785`). No seat, roster, floor or mailbox
     reaches a personal app's screens or storage.
   - **F.** But `declaresReplication` branches 15 times in `main.ts`,
     including on the personal share path (`sendingBytes`, `linkToSend`,
     `sendDocument`).
   - **F.** About 35 of the 77 registry codes are session, mailbox or merge
     codes (`src/refusals.ts`).
   - Which of those a personal app can surface: not verified.
   - **Smallest change:** a personal send/export path with no replication
     branch, plus a test that the personal sheet can produce only the two
     meanings.
7. **App-generated database changes, against host authority** (item 7).
   *Holds for personal apps; open for replicated ones.*
   - For a personal document the host derives nothing it owns from
     app-written state, except D36's `setup` flag.
   - For replicated documents, D196, D191 and the frame-supplied values
     listed in pass B (`handoff-2026-10-05.md`) stand.
   - **R.** A written rule, held by an invariant test: host decisions come
     from host storage, or from host SQL over base tables after a schema
     check. Never from app-writable views or tables.
8. **Strong isolation, against external services** (item 4). *Compatible*,
   as long as connectors are host-brokered `MessagePort`s (spec §4.7),
   never an ambient fetch. The real conflict today is WebRTC, which no page
   can close: a wording problem (Goal 6b).
9. **Self-contained artifacts, against evolving releases** (item 8).
   *Compatible.* Each release is an immutable signed set; evolution is a
   `supersedes` chain. The seam is meaning-preservation, which is
   contradiction 2's classifier.
10. **A universal format, against native-feeling capabilities** (item 10).
    *Compatible*, given named contracts and a required/optional split (§4.10).

**Engineering not needed for the V1.0 personal walk:**
- mailbox, push and badge
- session keys and per-game seals
- seats, rosters, `_rows`, floors
- invite filtering, D48
- the merge module and its pin
- person-key batch signing (personal documents never use it)
- the 123 merge vectors, P0–P2, the Rust reader
- the replicated branches through share and export

**R.** Keep it in CI. Do not extend it.

### 4. Required architecture specifications

Each spec gives: the invariant; who owns it; the interfaces and under whose
authority; failure behavior; acceptance evidence; what V1 excludes.

⚠ marks a contract that has two owners, or none, today.

#### 4.1 Working-copy identity and storage (Goals 1, 2)

- **Invariant.**
  - One working copy per (context, `documentUuid`).
  - One owner for its bytes.
  - "Saved" is shown only after the bytes and the revision commit under
    `dai:<uuid>`.
- **Owner.** The host (`apps/runner/src/opfs.ts`, the save path in
  `main.ts`). The runtime only reports.
  - ⚠ The bytes have two holders today: the stored database, and the
    library record's `html` (`main.ts:3599`). That is two answers to D51,
    and twice the quota.
- **Interfaces.** The frame's `dai:save` runs under standing authority. The
  host's acknowledgment is the only source of the status word.
- **Failure.** "Not saved". Data is kept in memory, retried, and Save a copy
  is offered.
- **Acceptance evidence.** WebKit e2e tests for:
  - an OPFS failure
  - a pagehide inside the debounce window
  - D51, asserting what is on screen
- **V1 exclusions.** Continuity across contexts or devices.
- **Identities.**
  - **F.** Each is kept separate:
    - document = `documentUuid`
    - release = `buildOf(manifest)` (`main.ts:3609`)
    - author = the person key, replicated documents only
    - storage = keyed by `documentUuid`
  - **I.** `documentUuid` means both "the app every recipient shares" and
    "this person's working copy". So one context holds exactly one working
    copy of an app.
  - **R.** Accept that for V1.0 and say so.

#### 4.2 Delivery and what a link means (Goal 1)

- **Invariant.**
  - A delivery names a document lineage and carries a snapshot.
  - On a device holding that lineage, it opens the working copy, whatever
    the carrier and whatever the network.
  - The snapshot is shown only on request ("View received copy").
- **Owner.** The host router (`start()`, `ingest`).
  - ⚠ Today the result depends on the carrier: `#a=` meets the invariant;
    `/d/` and `?open=` fetch first.
- **Failure.**
  - Copy held, delivery unreadable: open the copy, and say the link could
    not be read.
  - No copy held: one sentence and one next action (P3).
- **Acceptance evidence.** Per carrier: re-tap offline, and re-tap after
  expiry, on Chromium and WebKit.
- **V1 exclusions.** Cross-device continuity. Two working copies of one app
  in one context.

#### 4.3 Release succession and modification (Goals 3, 5)

- **Invariant.**
  - Records move to a new release only as a copy, under a change the host
    has classified.
  - The old release and its data stay intact and reopenable.
  - The original authorship is never rewritten.
- **Owner.** The host.
  - ⚠ Today three owners hold one decision: the shell computes the schema
    verdict (`bootloader.ts:3997`), the app frame runs the migration
    (`bootloader.ts:2468`), and the host decides adoption (`main.ts:4870`).
  - ⚠ Staging, activation and rollback have no owner.
- **Interfaces.** The pipeline is:
  1. a candidate (a bundle or a file)
  2. a host build
  3. the host classifier, on the two signed `runtime/schema.json` entries
     and never on SQL the app supplies:
     - same-schema: equal digests
     - reviewed additive: `ADD COLUMN … DEFAULT` and `CREATE TABLE IF NOT
       EXISTS` only
     - anything else: incompatible, and refused
  4. staging against a copy of the data
  5. a preview
  6. the host sheet "Use this version"
  7. a linked successor
- **Authority (to rule).** Adopt when one of these holds:
  - (a) it is signed by the predecessor's pinned publisher key; or
  - (b) it is signed by this context's person key, supersedes a document
    held here, and was accepted on the host sheet.
  - A successor under (b) is shown as this person's change. "Send the app"
    from it sends the modified release, with the publisher's original
    signature kept on its predecessor (ruling 7).
- **Failure.** Refuse in one sentence. Nothing is written under the old id,
  and the old app keeps working.
- **Acceptance evidence.** Value-level fixtures for each of:
  - values intact
  - the new field's default
  - the old signature still verifying
  - a failed migration changing nothing
  - rollback

  Counts are supporting evidence only.
- **V1 exclusions.** Renames, type changes, table splits, general
  migrations, and publisher push to every recipient.

#### 4.4 The two share meanings (Goal 4)

- **Invariant.**
  - "Send the app" = the signed set of the release this copy runs, plus an
    empty database. Starter content is signed seed SQL.
  - "Send this copy" = the flushed, saved database, with no residue of
    deleted rows.
  - No fallback changes the meaning chosen.
- **Owner.** The host (`currentHtml`, `sendDocument`, `exportContainer`).
  - ⚠ The fallback joins the two meanings at `main.ts:4669`.
- **Interfaces.** The app's `requestShare()` chooses the moment only. A
  session argument counts only for a signed `session` manifest.
- **Failure.** A failed link says so, and offers a file of the *same*
  meaning.
- **Acceptance evidence.**
  - Open each artifact and assert its rows: zero, or exactly the current
    ones.
  - Grep the bytes for a deleted sentinel.
- **V1 exclusions.** Invite.

#### 4.5 Host authority and isolation (Goal 6)

- **Invariant.**
  - App code reaches the host only through typed messages bound to its own
    mount.
  - It holds no key, no host storage, and no ambient network.
  - Its text is never shown as the host's.
  - Every act that crosses a boundary has a host sheet.
- **Owner.** The host runtime (the shell) and the opener.
- **Interfaces.**
  - Standing authority: save, flush, schema, insets, ground, used.
  - Host sheet: share, Save a copy, file export, remove, update, new key.
  - Typed protocol path: SIGN, for replicated documents only, re-derived by
    the host.
- **Failure.** Refuse, say a fixed host sentence, keep the work.
- **Acceptance evidence.** The existing isolation suite, plus new tests for:
  - app text in host chrome
  - a hosted download
  - a WebRTC run, so the limitations box rests on a run
- **V1 exclusions.**
  - WebRTC in browser hosts
  - old shells under file://
  - an app hurting its own user

#### 4.6 Key identity and signing contexts (Goals 5, 6)

- **Invariant.** One non-extractable person key per context. It is never
  exported, and never inherited by a restore.
- **Owner.** The host (`src/identity.ts`, `apps/runner/src/person.ts`).
  - **F.** The key is extractable today (`identity.ts:53`).
- **Interfaces.** The host signs only for the mount being written. Personal
  documents are not signed today (see 4.3 and §3 item 3).
- **Failure.** A lost key is a new context. A restored history keeps its
  signatures.
- **Acceptance evidence.**
  - `extractable === false`, asserted.
  - A restore fixture: the old author still verifies, and the new key
    writes the successor.
- **V1 exclusions.** Key export, recovery phrases.

#### 4.7 Shell and version compatibility (Goals 1, 6)

- **Invariant.** Every hosted open runs the current shell. This holds today
  (`main.ts:1528`).
- **Owner.** The host.
  - ⚠ `#a=` depends on host bytes retained from past deploys (`pastHosts`,
    `main.ts:616`). Dropping one breaks old links and icons.
- **Acceptance evidence.**
  - A link minted against the oldest retained host still opens.
  - A written retention rule.
- **V1 exclusions.** Old shells under file://, documented as such.

#### 4.8 Storage status words (Goals 2, 5)

- **Invariant.** Ruling 6's four states are shown from host evidence only:
  - **remembered here** = a library row
  - **saved here** = the last acknowledgment
  - **ready offline** = a recorded offline reopen in this context
  - **kept outside** = the date of an export the person made
- **Owner.** The host.
  - ⚠ Built: "Saved", and a persistence diagnostic beside the build stamp
    (`main.ts:960`). The other states are not built.
- **Acceptance evidence.** One test per state. "Ready offline" also needs a
  physical iPhone reading.

#### 4.9 AI authoring ↔ compiler and runtime (Goal 7)

- **Invariant.**
  - Every entry point (CLI build, MCP, the website paste box) either
    produces a file that runs and keeps entered data, or refuses with a
    finding id and a fix.
  - No entry point disagrees with `dai check --json`.
- **Owner.** `src/lint.ts` holds the single rule set. Each entry point
  enforces it.
  - ⚠ Today `dai build` and the paste box do not enforce it. MCP enforces a
    different fatal list (`src/mcp.ts:294-298`).
- **Interfaces.**
  - Input:
    - a JSON tool payload `{files, appName, supersedes?, upgradeOf?}`
    - a text bundle (`dai bundle v1`, with `document:`/`schema:` headers)
    - a zip
    - a single HTML file
  - Output: a `.dai.html`, or `{ok, findings[{id, file, what, why, fix}],
    advice[]}`.
- **Failure.**
  - Breaking findings are refused everywhere: network, browser storage,
    inline handlers, top-level await, no entry point.
  - A bundle wrapped in prose parses to exactly its files, or is refused.
  - A revision carrying `document:` supersedes that document, or is
    refused.
- **Acceptance evidence.**
  - Entry-point parity: the same fixtures through all three paths,
    including this review's P1, P2 and P3 outputs and the pasted bundle.
  - An opener drive: add an item, reload, the item is still present.
  - The 3.4 rate.
- **V1 exclusions.**
  - grammar-constrained bundles (decided 5 September)
  - device capabilities
  - no promise of success without the model file

#### 4.10 Future host-mediated capabilities (Part 6, feasibility only)

- **Without network access for app code?** Yes.
  - Spec §4.7 already decides on an unforgeable `MessagePort`, declared in
    the signed manifest, never network. `connect-src 'none'` stays.
  - Every hosted open is re-shelled, so a host can add the port without
    touching existing containers.
- **Credentials outside the document?** Yes. The host holds them in origin
  storage or a native keychain. The frame sees typed results only.
- **What an app declares.** Today's only signed declaration is `requires`,
  a sorted string list (`core.ts:1059-1061`).
  - A reader lacking a name must refuse (`container.ts:221-233`).
  - That is right for required capabilities, and wrong for an optional
    connector.
- **One contract, many hosts?** Yes, by name. A name never changes meaning
  (`docs/capabilities.md`); each host maps it to its own service.
- **A host without the capability.** A required name refuses, as today. An
  optional name should open, with the port answering "unavailable".
- **Offline.** Preserved if results are stored as ordinary rows and the
  port fails with typed errors.
- **What changes.**
  - Manifest: one signed field.
  - Bridge: one more public frame name. Only `FRAME_PUBLIC` names are
    frozen (`src/frame.ts:28`).
  - Container: none.
- **Authority.** Data an app hands to a connector crosses a boundary, so it
  takes a host sheet under ruling 9.
- **The decision to make now.**
  - **Ran:** `unknown manifest field 'connectors': ACCEPTED (unsigned)`.
    Unknown fields are accepted and not signed.
  - **F.** Verifiers rebuild the signed bytes from a fixed list of fields
    (`core.ts:985-1070`).
  - **I.** So a future writer that signs a new field fails every reader
    deployed today as "not authentic", unless `manifestVersion` is bumped.
  - **R.** Choose one, before public V1.0 readers ship:
    - a reserved, signed optional list (for example `uses: string[]`:
      verified, and names the reader lacks are ignored)
    - a signed extension map
    - a written commitment that every new signed field is a version bump

    Then wire the choice into `signedViewOf`/`signedBytes`, the Python
    reader, the CDDL, and a vector. It is cheap now and breaking later.

## Owner/author spike

Branch `spike/owner-author`, from main at `4303838c`. It is never merged.
Probe code is under `spikes/2026-10-18-owner-author/`. No file under
`src/`, `apps/runner/src/` or `docs/` changed except this section. Nothing
was written to the production store or the relay.

The direction under test:
- The working copy's durable home is an encrypted, versioned store, held
  by an **owner**, who can recover the document and admit contexts to it.
- Each context signs as its own **author**, with its own key.
- One person may be many authors.

### Results

- **A. Pass.** One key in two contexts is equivocation at the first
  concurrent offline write. Both copies report `AUTHOR_EQUIVOCATED`, and
  both lose both rows.
- **B(i). Pass.** Two keys, different rows: both copies converge, nothing
  is lost, and both readers agree.
- **B(ii). A reading.** Two keys, the same row: both copies converge on

### C and D: the probe and the phone

**Where it ran.**
- **F.** The preview deployment could not host the probe:
  - Its URLs answer `302` to `vercel.com/sso-api`.
  - The installed app keeps its own cookies, so it would meet that
    sign-in in the middle of the reading.
  - Its store is production's.
- **F.** The probe ran instead as its own Cloudflare Worker,
  **`dai-spike-owner`**, at
  **https://dai-spike-owner.opendai.workers.dev**.
  - It has one Durable Object, and no binding shared with the relay or
    the store.
  - It is deleted after this section is recorded (below).

**What the probe does.**
- **Owner.** A platform passkey. The store checks every ceremony itself:
  origin, RP id hash, UP and UV, and the ES256 signature.
- **Authors.** Each context has its own non-extractable ES256 author key
  in IndexedDB. The author id is SHA-256(raw key)[0:16], as
  `authorIdOf`. A context enrolls under the owner with a proof of
  possession.
- **A write:**
  1. pulls the latest checkpoint;
  2. merges rows by id;
  3. appends one signed row;
  4. encrypts the database under a fresh AES-GCM key;
  5. wraps that key under PRF, the keychain secret (each by HKDF to
     AES-KW) and a store-held copy;
  6. uploads a checkpoint `{release, document, revision, database,
     previous}`, signed by the author. The store refuses a checkpoint that
     does not extend the owner's latest.
- **The store-held copy** is a control. It keeps C's question (do the rows
  survive through the owner?) apart from D's (does a client-held key
  survive?).
- **What the Worker held: plaintext, in effect.**
  - **F.** The database went up as AES-GCM ciphertext, and each
    checkpoint's envelope carried the data key itself, unwrapped:
    `envelope.store = { key: <raw AES key, base64url> }`
    (`probe/public/app.js:281-283`).
  - **F.** The Worker stored the envelope beside the ciphertext in the
    same record (`probe/worker.js`, `POST /api/checkpoint`) and served
    both back on `GET /api/checkpoint/latest`. Anything holding the
    Durable Object's storage could open every checkpoint.
  - **F.** No restore in the readings used that path. Each one names
    `"openedBy":"prf"`, with the store path listed only as `available
    (control)`.
  - C proved recovery; D proved key recoverability; their composition is
    the first build's red test.
- **Fingerprints shown** are SHA-256("fp:" ‖ secret)[0:8], never the
  secret.

### What each result decides

- **A. A shared author key stays rejected.**
  - Demonstrated on the runtime and the reference reader.
  - The first concurrent offline write in two contexts makes the person an
    equivocator in their own document, and loses both writes on every copy.
  - Under a session profile, it also costs every seat they hold
    (`#equivocator-holds-nothing`).
- **B. Sessionless multi-author replication can be the V1.2 engine for one
  person's contexts.**
  - Different rows converge with nothing lost.
  - The one-row conflict is the open part: both values are kept and
    signed, and `_current` shows one, chosen by author-id order on a clock
    tie.
  - **R.** Before it carries a person's own copies, the conflict must be
    shown by default and not left to each app: the kit marks a row changed
    in two places and offers both. Otherwise a person's two devices
    silently pick a winner by key order.
- **C. Owner recovery works on the phone.**
  - A passkey in iCloud Keychain serves Safari and the installed app, and
    outlives a website-data wipe.
  - Through it, rows from two authors came back with their author ids
    intact.
  - A wiped context is a new author, as §4.6 wants: "a lost key is a new
    context". Four authors were enrolled under one owner in one sitting,
    so enrollment must be cheap and the author list unbounded per owner.
  - **F.** The installed app's storage is separate from Safari's, both
    ways: a Safari clear left it whole. This is a reading for §4.1's
    two-context rule, and for the go/no-go's iPhone condition.
- **D. PRF is the dependable wrapping path; the keychain password is a
  fallback.**
  - PRF on the owner passkey gave the same secret in both contexts and
    after the wipe, with no typing. A wrapping key derived from it opened
    every checkpoint either context wrote.
  - The keychain password also survived. But it needs a form submission to
    be saved (there is no `PasswordCredential` in Safari), and a manual
    fill each time.
  - **A.** This is one phone, one Apple Account and one passkey provider.
    Not tested: PRF with other providers, Android, and a deleted or
    replaced passkey (which loses the PRF secret). A second wrapping path
    is still needed for that case.
